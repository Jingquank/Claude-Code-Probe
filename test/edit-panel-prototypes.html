Cells, rows, or both: the panel at 13px with one stepper

Four structures for the same panel, each carrying every state the plan lists, on the +1 type scale by default. Inside each, three ways of showing that a value sits on a token. The width and size switches in the top bar move all four at once, so structure, form, size and width can be judged one at a time.

Where it stands

  • Flat hierarchy: group titles and labels are both 11px in the secondary tone.
  • Two languages: a tracked-caps grid for Typography, sentence-case rows for the rest.
  • A column of empty rings, one per row, for a mark most rows never earn.
  • Three control widths, so the right edge is not an edge.
  • The checkerboard shows as a frame around opaque colours.
  • 11px is the working size; units, tokens and the footer sit at 9 to 10.

What holds in all four

Type. Labels and values at 13, section titles at 14 medium, units and captions at 12, keyboard chips at 11. Sans for words, mono for values. Sentence case everywhere; the tracked caps are gone.

The accent means two things. A value that exists in the source — a token name, in mono — and a property that has been edited: the label turns accent and is the reset, a small ↺ beside it on hover. Plus the one primary action, copy. Nothing else is coloured.

Fields read as fields. Two derived tokens, --pnt-field and --pnt-field-border, mixed from surface-2 and the ground, so no theme block changes. Units are quieter than numbers but live inside the chip. The swatch has no padding ring: an opaque colour fills it edge to edge, and the checkerboard shows only through translucency.

One stepper. The chip's ends carry a faint pair of chevrons at rest — the hint that it scrubs — which come up on hover and are the ‹ › stepper wherever a ladder exists, in rows and cells alike. The wheel only scrolls. ⌥↑↓ walks the ladder from the keyboard.

The dot column is gone. Rows and cells lift one step on hover; inputs carry a visible focus ring; a scrub tints the chip.

Header and footer. Two drawn icons at one stroke weight, copy in the accent with its count; a grip appears when the header is hovered, because the header is the drag handle; the degraded marker is a 12px ring whose reason appears on hover. The footer is 12px, its chips 11.

Capsules truncate. A token's name can be longer than its capsule — --spacing-horizontal-m ends in an ellipsis in a two-column cell at 280 — so every capsule carries a tooltip with the full name, drawn in the tooltip root the selection layouts already use, above the capsule and centred, below it when there is no room. The rung list never truncates: it is as wide as its longest name.

01

Cells everywhere

Every group is a grid of labelled cells — the label above the value, three across for Typography, two across for everything else. One label register, one mark, one field, and the right edge is the column edge by construction. Disclosure is a caret row at the end of a group; sides split into four cells under their parent; Advanced is a caret group like the others. Beside it, the colour picker and the long-text editor in the same register, and — on form 3 — the rung list.

Wins
One language for the whole panel, and the right edge stops being a problem: a cell is as wide as its column, so every value, colour and segment ends on the same line. The +1 scale fits because the label is above the value, not beside it.
Costs
A three-column cell is 80px at 280 and 75px at 264, which is where form 1's token name starts to truncate — title-sm just fits, --wave-amp does not. Form 2 has to stack its capsule under the chip, which is a second deck.
Height
Roughly what the mixed panel is today: Typography loses two rows to the grid, Spacing gains one for the split sides. Shown at 264/280 it clears a 900px viewport with Advanced open.
Risk
The label above the value reads as a form, and a scanned inspector wants a column of names. Squint at Spacing and Size: are the labels still findable, or do the chips take over?

Rows everywhere

It is the most scannable of the four and the tallest — so it is shown scrolled, with the header and footer shadows that say there is more.

03

Hybrid, one language

Rows for four groups, cells for Typography, and one of everything else: one label register, one edited mark, one field, the caret row for every disclosure, one stepper. Why is Typography special? Because it is the only group whose values are read together — a type style is six properties that mean one thing, and the grid puts them side by side where a column would make you read them in sequence. Spacing, size, surface and shadow are lists of independent dials, and a list wants rows.

Choosing

  1. Squint. With the detail blurred, does each panel read title, then label, then value, in that order? A structure where the chips win the squint has the hierarchy upside down.
  2. Does the token name read as source or as decoration? Switch form 1 → 2 → 3 on one panel. The accent should say "this exists in the code", not "this is highlighted". Watch p-4 beside 16, and --ink beside a hex.
  3. Which form survives auto? The width and height rows carry a second control already. Form 2 adds a third; see whether the row still lines up with the others.
  4. 12 or 13, at 264 or 280. Flip both switches. At 12/264 the token names fit in every cell and the panel feels like today's; at 13/280 it reads from a chair back, and title-sm is the first name to truncate in a three-column cell.
  5. Two ladders, one panel. Size is on a token, leading is on page values. On each form, can you tell which is which without hovering? That difference is the whole point of ADR 0004.
