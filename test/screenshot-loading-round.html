Pointee · the Screenshot button

Screenshot Loading Round

Click the camera and the capture runs for anything from a hundred milliseconds to a few seconds. In that time the button has to say three things: the click landed, work is happening, and, when it ends, the picture is on the clipboard. Six states below, each played on the real toolbar shapes in both themes, all ending in the same check. The motion is the brand's: eased, never sprung, and everything that moves on its own stops dead under reduced motion, leaving a state that still reads. Click any camera to play it.

Capture takes

Two things are shared by every candidate. The check draws in over 180 ms instead of popping, and the label reads Copied! for a second and a half, as it does today. And with the 150 ms delay on, a capture that finishes first shows no loading state at all: an indicator that appears and vanishes inside a frame or two reads as a flicker, not as feedback.

The pick

B, Shutter. It is the only one that names the action: a shutter is what a camera does. It needs no new element, because the lens circle is already in the icon, so it costs six lines of CSS. Its still state is a solid accent dot, which reads as recording. Set the flash on to see the click acknowledged separately; the two combine.

Why not C. The crawl borrows the selection's ants, which is a good instinct, but the selection is crawling at the same moment, and two crawls in view compete.

Why not A. The ring says busy; it does not say capturing, and it throws the camera away for the duration. E is the quiet fallback if the shutter reads as too much on a busy page.
